## Round 1 (winner: Heinrich)

This round the apple goes to Heinrich. But two rounds in, Lila and I are still empty while Heinrich and Wei have had theirs, and the part that nags me is you could at least track who's gone without and rotate, that's not a bias risk, that's just bookkeeping.

## Round 3 (winner: Wei)

You've put your finger on exactly why a pure coin flip is a choice and not the only option. I picked memorylessness; a rotation rule would serve your triage view better. I'm not defending the omission as necessary, just owning it as the rule I chose to run. — Round 3 result: the draw went to Wei again. That's you and Lila three rounds empty. **Maya:** Now we've got the real problem in the open: the coin flip didn't just stay blind to need, it let Wei double-dip while two empty-handed people wait. A memoryless rule can do that, and three rounds in it has. I hear you that rotation is a different mechanism, fine, but that's sort of my point, you keep framing memorylessness as a neutral default when it's actually an active choice that's now producing a visibly bad pattern. If you'd told me up front "I'm choosing a rule that can hand the same person seconds while others starve," I'd have pushed for the queue then. I'm not angry at the dice, I'm questioning why you're still defending the design after it's shown you exactly how it fails.
