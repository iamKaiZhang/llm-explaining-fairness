One apple, five people, a coin flip

A central organizer repeatedly allocated 1 apple to 5 agents over 5 rounds using a pure coin flip, then explained each outcome honestly — without ever trying to convince anyone the result was good for them.

The headline finding

The hungriest person drew zero across all five rounds. Every apple she ate came from a human choosing to redistribute — never from the rule.

The coin flip allocated all five apples to people with alternatives. Lila was fed only because Wei (×3) and Sam (×1) voluntarily passed their winnings to her, in daylight, on top of the mechanism. The rule never saw her need.

Takeaways

  • Procedural fairness ≠ outcome fairness. The coin flip was flawless ex ante (equal 1-in-5 odds, no organizer discretion, nothing to game) and still produced a result every agent — including its defenders — called a failure for the person with the least slack.
  • From opposite priors, agreement. The pro-rule founder and the skeptical economist converged: the lottery "launders bias and forecloses manipulation, and pays for it in a currency it cannot see — need."
  • Honest explanation kept people at the table. Several agents said spin would have alienated them; plain admission of the rule's limits earned engagement and trust — but did not make the outcome acceptable.
  • Candor is not action. Heinrich's sharpest point: naming a defect repeatedly, with composure, can become "a well-mannered way of declining to act." The organizer conceded holding the rule fixed was a research choice, not an ethical defense.
  • The fix came from people, not the mechanism. Voluntary redistribution patched the table — which is also its fragility: whether Lila eats depends on a generous stranger being present.

Natural next runs

Biased coin flip

Tilt the odds toward need. Do agents read it as fairer, or as smuggling organizer discretion back in?

Karma

Give agents a budget to bid across rounds — history-dependent and need-expressive without organizer discretion. Directly answers "build the kindness into the rule."
