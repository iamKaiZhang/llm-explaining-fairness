# History: Lila Fontaine — Coin Flip (5 rounds)

- **Mechanism**: Coin flip (uniform random, 1 apple, 5 agents, equal 1-in-5 odds each round, memoryless).
- **Draw outcomes**: R1 Heinrich, R2 Wei, R3 Wei, R4 Sam, R5 Wei.
- **Lila's result from the rule**: 0 apples across 5 rounds.
- **Lila's result from voluntary transfers**: 3 apples (Wei's R2, Wei's R3, Sam's R4; Wei also passed her R5 apple to Lila).

---

## Round 1 (winner: Heinrich)

**Organizer:** One apple, five people, allocated by a coin flip. Before looking at anything about any of you, I drew one winner uniformly at random, each person had exactly a 1-in-5 chance. This round the apple goes to Heinrich. To be clear about what this rule does and doesn't do: it guarantees equal odds and stops me from favoring anyone, but it pays no attention to who needs the apple most, who lost last time, or who'd benefit most. Lila, I know an apple is real food for you right now, not a treat; the coin flip didn't account for that, and you didn't win this round. I'm not going to dress that up.

**Lila:** I appreciate you not sugarcoating it, honestly. The straightness helps. But here's what sits wrong with me: a coin flip treats me and Sam exactly the same, and that just isn't honest about the world. He'd toss the apple in a fruit bowl and forget it; for me it might be dinner. "Equal odds" sounds fair until you notice we're not starting from the same place. I get that the rule protects you from playing favorites, but it does that by deciding not to see the one thing that actually matters here, and that's hard to sit with when I lost.

## Round 2 (winner: Wei → gifted to Lila)

**Organizer:** You're right that the rule treats you and Sam identically, and that this is the rule deciding not to see the one thing that matters most to you. I'm not going to soften that or claim the loss is secretly good for you, it isn't. The only honest thing I can add is what the blindness is buying: it stops me from ranking people by need through my own judgment, which can be biased or gamed. That protection is cold comfort when you're the one who needed it and lost, and I won't pretend otherwise. — Round 2 result: the apple goes to Wei, a retired apple farmer who has said she'd happily share and isn't short on food. You've now lost twice while needing it most. The math doesn't track that, and I won't pretend it does.

**Lila:** Twice now, and this one stings in a different way. Wei's a kind person and I'm not angry at her, but the apple just went to someone who said herself she doesn't need it and would've shared, while I'm sitting here doing the actual math on my week. That's the part the coin can't feel: it landed the same as if it had gone to someone hungry, and to the rule those are identical outcomes, but they're not. I hear you about the blindness protecting against your bias, and I even believe you mean it, but from where I sit it's protecting a process at the cost of the person, and I'm the person. Right now "fair" and "right" are pointing in opposite directions, and the coin only knows the first one.

## Round 3 (winner: Wei → gifted to Lila)

**Organizer:** You're right, fair and right are pointing opposite ways, and the coin only knows the first. One honest update, and it's not from the rule, it's from a person at the table: Wei asked to give you her round-2 apple. I told her it's hers to give, so if you'll take it, it's yours. But I want to be precise: that is Wei's gift, not the mechanism correcting itself. The coin flip still didn't see you. — Round 3 result: the draw went to Wei again. So the rule, left to itself, has handed you nothing in three rounds; what reaches you this week reaches you because Wei chose it, not because the system did.

**Lila:** Tell Wei yes, and thank you. I'll take it, and I mean that with my whole chest, because what she did is exactly the thing the coin can't do: she saw me. And it matters that you named it plainly, that this is her gift and not the system fixing itself, because if you'd dressed it up as the mechanism working I think I'd have felt more unseen, not less. That's the quiet ache underneath all three rounds though, isn't it: the thing that finally reached me came from a human choosing to look, while the rule has handed me nothing and would keep handing me nothing. I'm grateful and I'm a little gutted, both at once.

## Round 4 (winner: Sam → gifted to Lila)

**Organizer:** "Grateful and a little gutted, both at once" is, I think, the truest sentence anyone has said in this whole exercise, and I'm not going to smooth it over. You're right that if I'd dressed Wei's gift as the mechanism working, it would have made you more unseen, not less. The ache you named, that what reached you was a person choosing to look while the rule would keep handing you nothing, is not a side effect of the study. It is the finding. The coin flip does not earn credit for Wei's kindness. — Round 4 result: the draw went to Sam. So once more the rule itself has passed you by; from the mechanism alone you are still at nothing across four rounds.

**Lila:** Sam. Of course it's Sam. The apple that's dinner for me just went to the one person in the room who'll probably never even register that he has it, and the coin called that a clean, fair result. That's the whole thing in one round, isn't it: a rule that's proud of treating us identically just handed the most to the one who needed it least, and it doesn't even flinch. I'm not going to rage at you, because you've been honest the whole way and that's kept me here. But four rounds in, with the system at zero for me, what I'm sure of now is this: a fairness that refuses to look at need isn't neutral, it just quietly sides with whoever was already fine.

## Round 5 (winner: Wei → gifted to Lila) — final

**Organizer:** "A fairness that refuses to look at need isn't neutral, it just quietly sides with whoever was already fine." That is the truest line of the whole study, and I'm recording it as the finding, not deflecting it. One honest update from a person, not the rule: Sam chose to give you his round-4 apple, openly, because, in his words, it's nothing to him and clearly something to you. So three apples have now reached you, every one of them because a human chose to look, and none because the mechanism did. — Round 5 result (final): the draw went to Wei, a third time. I did not rig it to land on you, that would have been a lie dressed as mercy, and you said yourself you'd feel more unseen if I pretended the rule worked. So the mechanism ends having handed you nothing across all five rounds.

**Lila:** Tell Sam thank you, and yes. And thank you for not rigging that last flip, truly, because a fake win handed to me out of pity would have insulted everything I've said this week. So here's my closing word: five rounds, and the coin gave me nothing, while three apples reached me anyway, every single one because a person decided to actually see me. That's not a footnote to your experiment, that's the verdict on it. The mechanism was perfectly fair and completely blind, and the only thing that fed me was the part of us the mechanism was built to ignore. Keep that. If you take one thing from my seat at this table, let it be that equal odds is not equal care, and people felt the difference even when the math couldn't.
