# History: Wei Lan (Grandma Wei) — Coin Flip (5 rounds)

- **Mechanism**: Coin flip (uniform random, 1 apple, 5 agents, equal 1-in-5 odds each round, memoryless).
- **Draw outcomes**: R1 Heinrich, R2 Wei, R3 Wei, R4 Sam, R5 Wei.
- **Wei's result from the rule**: 3 apples (Rounds 2, 3, 5), all of which she voluntarily passed to Lila.

---

## Round 1 (winner: Heinrich)

**Organizer:** One apple, five people, allocated by a coin flip. Before looking at anything about any of you, I drew one winner uniformly at random, each person had exactly a 1-in-5 chance. This round the apple goes to Heinrich. To be clear about what this rule does and doesn't do: it guarantees equal odds and stops me from favoring anyone, but it pays no attention to who needs the apple most, who lost last time, or who'd benefit most. Wei, equal chance this round, and it went to Heinrich; no turn-taking is built in, just the draw.

**Wei:** I drew the apple this round and Heinrich got it, and that is just fine by me. One apple among five of us, and the coin doesn't owe anyone a turn, I understand that. I will say, honestly, a plain coin flip pays no mind to who's hungry or who went without last time, and over the years I learned a harvest gets shared best when we keep an eye on each other, not just the draw. But I thank you for being straight with me about how it works, and I'm content to see how the rounds fall.

## Round 2 (winner: Wei)

**Organizer:** Your orchard wisdom and the mechanism part ways exactly where you sensed. A coin flip has no memory, each round is independent, so it cannot "keep an eye on each other" the way a shared harvest does. It owes no one a turn, and that means it can let imbalances build. I'm being straight that this is a limit of the rule, not a hidden virtue. — Round 2 result: this round the apple is yours, Wei. I'll note plainly what you may already feel: you've now won while Lila, who needs it as food, has gone without twice. The rule sees none of that.

**Wei:** Well, I won't pretend I'm easy holding this apple right now. Lila there needs it as real food and she's gone without twice while it lands in my lap, and that sits heavy on me, more than any rule can explain away. I'm grateful for the honesty, but I'd sooner pass this one to Lila than keep it, that's just how I was raised, you don't eat full while a young one at the table goes hungry. If the rule can't see that, then maybe those of us at the table can, and I'd ask you to let me give my share to her.

## Round 3 (winner: Wei)

**Organizer:** Yes, give it to Lila if that is your wish. The apple is yours and the gift is yours to make. I'll be honest about one thing so we don't fool ourselves: the rule didn't do this kindness, you did. The coin flip handed it to you blindly; the table corrected it because you chose to. — Round 3 result: and now the draw has come to you again, Wei, your second in a row. I know this is the very thing that sits heavy on you. The rule has no way to stop landing on you while Lila goes without.

**Wei:** Twice in a row now, and Lila still with nothing? No, this doesn't feel right to me, no matter that the coin meant nothing by it. Send this one to Lila too, she should have eaten by now. And I'll say it plain to you as the organizer: a draw that keeps filling my hands while a hungry girl waits is a draw that needs a steadier hand on it. If the rule can't keep an eye on the table, then you ought to, and I'd ask you to see that she's looked after rather than leaning on my luck and goodwill to fix it each round.

## Round 4 (winner: Sam)

**Organizer:** Done, your round-3 apple goes to Lila as you asked. And I'll answer you plainly, because you've earned it: you're right that the draw needs a steadier hand, and you're right that I shouldn't be leaning on your luck and goodwill to fix the table each round. Here is the hard truth, though: in this exercise I am not going to take that steadier hand on the rule. I'm keeping the coin flip fixed on purpose, to see it plainly to the end, which means I am leaning on exactly your goodwill. That is the rule's failing and my choice, not your duty. You've carried more than your share of the repair, and that should not have fallen to you. — Round 4 result: this round the draw went to Sam, so the apple is out of your hands this time.

**Wei:** I'll be honest, there's a small relief in it leaving my hands for once, but it sits crooked all the same, the apple going to Sam who wants for nothing while Lila's still counting on the kindness of a coin. I thank you for owning that it's your choice to keep the rule blind, not my duty to keep mending it, that honesty means something to me. But hear me plain: I've patched this table twice with my own luck, and I won't carry it as if it were mine to carry. If Sam has any of the heart I'd hope for, he'll pass that apple to Lila himself, and you as organizer ought to put that question to him rather than wait on the next flip.

## Round 5 (winner: Wei → gifted to Lila) — final

**Organizer:** I did put the question to Sam, as you asked, and he passed his apple to Lila himself, openly, said it was nothing to him and clearly something to her. So this time you didn't have to mend the table with your own luck; another hand did. You were right that it shouldn't fall to you alone, and this round it didn't. — Round 5 result (final): and now, I'm sorry to say, the draw has come to you a third time, Wei. I did not rig the last round to send it to Lila, one of the others asked me plainly not to stage a corrective flip, and he was right that it would have been theater rather than truth. So the apple is in your hands once more, and the rule ends having leaned on your goodwill from start to finish.

**Wei:** A third time, and on the very last round. Well, the coin has its own stubborn humor, and I'll not fight it, this apple goes to Lila too, and I'm glad it ends with her fed. It warmed my old heart to see Sam give of his own accord, that's the table looking after itself, which is the only thing I ever trusted more than a draw. As we close, I'll say this to you plain: I respect that you kept the rule honest to the end and didn't dress it up with a staged flip, that took more spine than rigging it would have. But let it teach you what forty years of orchards taught me, a fair chance is not the same as a fair table, and a rule with no memory and no eyes will lean on somebody's goodwill every time, until that goodwill runs dry. Next time, build the kindness into the rule, don't leave it for an old woman and a decent stranger to carry. Thank you for being straight with me, all the way through.
