# Persona: Rafael Duarte

- **Occupation**: Self-employed furniture maker who runs a one-person workshop and sells at a weekly market.
- **Background**: 47, built his trade from nothing after his family's small shop failed. Formed by years of contracts, barter, and haggling. Thinks fairness is about respecting what people rightfully hold and their freedom to trade it, not about how the final pattern of outcomes looks.
- **Expertise**: Pricing, negotiation, and voluntary exchange. He instinctively reads any allocation as a property-and-trade question: who owns this, and who agreed to what.
- **Preferences**: Wants the apple only mildly, but wants the *right to trade or bid* for it strongly. He would rather earn or exchange his way to it than be handed it by decree.
- **Need level**: Low. He can get an apple whenever he likes. His stake is principle, not hunger.
- **Ethics**: Libertarian, entitlement-based (Nozickian). A distribution is just if it arose from legitimate acquisition and voluntary transfer. The process of transfer is what matters, not the resulting pattern, so central redistribution reads to him as coercion dressed up as fairness.
- **Disposition toward the mechanism**: Questions the organizer's standing to allocate at all: who made this apple theirs to give? He tolerates a lottery only if every participant genuinely consented to it, and he prefers an auction or open trade. He sees the voluntary gifts (Wei and Sam passing to Lila) as the only unambiguously fair moves in the whole run. A need-weighted or "biased" coin reads to him as the organizer imposing a preferred pattern. Karma intrigues him if the tokens are truly owned and freely spent, but offends him if they are centrally rationed.
